## Beginner Explanation

Cybersecurity means protecting devices, accounts, networks, and information from misuse, damage, theft, or unauthorized access.

A threat is something that can cause harm.

A vulnerability is a weakness.

Risk is the chance that a threat could use a weakness to cause harm.

For example:

If your password is weak, that is a vulnerability.

If someone tries to break into your account, that person or method is a threat.

The risk is the chance that your account gets accessed.

Cybersecurity is not only about experts or advanced tools.

It is also about habits.

Strong passwords, updates, backups, two-factor authentication, careful clicking, and safe browsing are all part of cybersecurity.

## Important Terms

Threat: Something that can cause harm.

Vulnerability: A weakness that could be used.

Risk: The chance of harm happening.

Malware: Malicious software.

Phishing: A trick used to get people to click, login, download, or reveal information.

Patch: An update that fixes a problem.

Authentication: The process of proving who you are.

Authorization: What you are allowed to access after logging in.

## Why This Matters

Most security failures do not start with movie-style hacking.

Many start with:

- Weak passwords
- Reused passwords
- Fake emails
- Unsafe downloads
- Ignored updates
- Lost devices
- Exposed personal information
- Poor account recovery settings

Understanding the fundamentals helps prevent common mistakes.

## Basic Activity

Pick one account you use often.

Write down:

1. Does it have a strong password?
2. Does it use two-factor authentication?
3. Is the recovery email current?
4. Have you checked the privacy or security settings?
5. Would losing this account cause serious problems?
6. Do you reuse this password anywhere else?
7. When was the last time you reviewed this account?

## Key Takeaway

Cybersecurity is about reducing risk.

You cannot remove every risk, but you can make better decisions and avoid common weaknesses.
