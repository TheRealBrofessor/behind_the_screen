# Networking Basics

## Goal

The goal of this section is to explain how devices communicate with each other.

Networking is one of the most important foundations in cybersecurity.

## What You Will Learn

- What a network is
- What an IP address is
- What a router does
- What DNS does
- What ports are
- What packets are
- The difference between LAN and WAN
- Why networking matters in security

## Beginner Explanation

A network is a group of devices that can communicate.

Your phone, laptop, router, smart TV, and printer may all be part of your home network.

An IP address is like a device address on a network.

A router helps devices connect to each other and to the internet.

DNS helps turn website names into IP addresses. When you type a website name, DNS helps your device find where that website lives.

Ports are like numbered doors used by different services.

Packets are small pieces of data that move across a network.

Cybersecurity depends on networking because attacks, defenses, websites, apps, logins, and online activity all involve communication between devices.

## Important Terms

LAN: Local Area Network. This is usually your home, school, or workplace network.

WAN: Wide Area Network. The internet is the biggest example.

IP Address: A number used to identify a device on a network.

Router: A device that moves traffic between networks.

DNS: The system that matches names like example.com to IP addresses.

Port: A numbered entry point used by services.

Packet: A small unit of network data.

## Why This Matters

If you understand networking, you can better understand:

- How websites load
- How devices talk
- How defenders monitor traffic
- Why routers matter
- Why public Wi-Fi can be risky
- Why logs and alerts matter
